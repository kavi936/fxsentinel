The UK’s preliminary GDP growth rate for the quarter came in at **0.3%**, exceeding expectations of **0.1%** but down from the previous **0.7%**. GDP measures the inflation-adjusted value of goods and services produced in the economy and is a crucial indicator of economic performance.
**Why it matters:**

* Stronger GDP = stronger economy → potential delay in rate cuts → bullish for GBP.
* Weaker GDP = slower economy → more easing expectations → bearish for GBP.

In this case, the better-than-expected reading is considered **mildly positive** for sterling as it signals resilience despite a broader slowdown.

---

### **Immediate Market Impact**

The immediate reaction to this surprise has been **GBP bullish**, as the figure significantly beat expectations. Short-term volatility has increased, with potential moves of **40–70 pips in GBP/USD** during the initial reaction window. Primary impact will be seen in **GBP/USD, EUR/GBP, and GBP/JPY**, while UK gilt yields may edge higher as rate-cut bets diminish.
**Key drivers of reaction:**

* Positive growth surprise vs market expectations.
* Repricing of BoE rate-cut timing.

---

### **Long-Term Implications**

This data point supports a **narrative of UK economic resilience** and reduces the urgency for near-term monetary easing by the Bank of England. While growth is slower than the previous quarter, the beat relative to consensus suggests a soft landing scenario rather than a sharp contraction. If upcoming inflation and labor market data also come in strong, sterling could maintain its upward bias over the coming weeks.
